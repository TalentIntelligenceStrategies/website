# Hero-Video Storyboards (narration-first)

> **Why this exists.** The homepage opens on a 3-slide carousel that loses cold visitors. The plan: kill it, replace with one hero that tells **one** story — delivered as a ~30-second video. Before building, we want to *read the story aloud* and decide if it's right. Each storyboard below forces one candidate story down to a single speakable throughline. (These candidate stories originated in the now-deleted `strategic-direction.md` §3 and are self-contained here; current positioning SoT is [brand/positioning.md](../../brand/positioning.md).)
>
> **How to use it.** Read each **narration column top to bottom, aloud, at a natural pace.** The winner is the one where, in ~30 seconds, a stranger grasps all four: **what TIS is · what it does · why TIS · who TIS is.** Then lock it (or a one-line swap) and the homepage brief begins. Visuals are *direction*, not final — the video tool is still TBD.
>
> **Status (2026-06-19):** **#1 is LOCKED as a silent, on-screen-text film (no voiceover)** — see the v4 beat sheet, built line by line with the user. Storyboards **2–6 remain VO-style sketches** kept as alternates; the VO/length notes below describe those sketches, not the locked #1.

## Shared setup (applies to every storyboard)

- **Length:** ~30s · ~5 scenes · ~55–70 words of narration (VO).
- **The backbone every version follows:** `Hook (attention) → What TIS is → What it does (the moves/pillars) → Why TIS (the differentiator) → CTA`. This is what guarantees the four newcomer questions get answered.
- **Tone:** 泰然 — composed, evidence-driven, anti-hype — **dialed up to bold/cinematic for the hero moment.** Bigger than body copy, still truthful. No false urgency.
- **Visual treatment:** abstract **motion-graphics primary** (kinetic type, patents as nodes, the engine as a structured grid, SABCD grades resolving), with an *optional mixed b-roll / product-shot* note per beat for when we want concreteness. Written to work either way.
- **Brand fidelity (non-negotiable):**
  - On-screen text renders anchor numbers **verbatim**: `180M-patent database`, `3rd-largest globally`, `SABCD`, `50 indicators`. (VO may use natural-language equivalents per brand-voice §6's marketing-prose exception.)
  - No banned phrases ("AI-powered," "revolutionary," "disruptive," "unlock," "seamless," "leverage," "empower," "actionable," "solutions").
  - **Innovue** appears only as the credential behind the data ("powered by Innovue" / "Innovue's 180M-patent database"), never as co-lead.
  - Public wording: **"patent owner & licensor"** posture — never "NPE." Pillar 1 stays unnamed (placeholder). Sector stays agnostic — no drones/UAV.

### Production spec — v4 film *(Higgsfield plates + post type-track)*

**How this works (read first).** Higgsfield (and Runway/Kling/Sora-class tools) generate **short ~3–6s clips**, not a finished 30s film, and they **mangle on-screen text**. So this film is built in two layers:

1. **PLATE layer** — generate each plate below in Higgsfield as a **silent, text-free background clip**. Best results: generate a still **start frame** first (image), then animate it with the motion/camera note. Stitch the plates in order in an editor (Premiere / After Effects / CapCut).
2. **TYPE layer** — composite the kinetic Urbanist type *on top* of the stitched plates in post, on the timecodes in the type-track. This is where the per-reveal granularity lives, and it's the only way to keep `180M-patent database` / `OWN·LICENSE·INVEST` spelled correctly and on-brand.
3. **TRANSITIONS** — the "in between" each plate is specced explicitly below; apply in the editor.

**Global generation settings (apply to every plate prompt):**
- **Canvas:** **clean soft off-white digital backdrop `#F7F4EF`** with a smooth subtle gradient — bright, airy, studio-lit, generous negative space. **No texture, no paper, no grain.** **Aspect:** 16:9 (1920×1080); generate a 9:16 + 1:1 pass later for social.
- **Style tokens:** clean **abstract data-network** motion-graphics, modern premium tech / SaaS aesthetic, sharp, precise, minimal, studio-lit, shallow depth of field, 24fps, slow deliberate camera. **Digital, not handcraft.**
- **Treatment rule:** **structure = crisp glowing dark `#252525` data-nodes + thin precise vector connecting lines** (clean network graph / grid); **objects = clean crisp rectangular panels** (light panels, thin dark borders, soft subtle drop shadows). **Central anchor = the TIS isometric cube** — a solid dark charcoal cube, strict 30/60/90-degree construction, plain clean faces (the real logo's T/I/S monogram + notch is composited only at the end; never generated).
- **Color:** **monochrome** (dark charcoal on soft off-white) throughout; the cube's edges carry a *subtle cool-to-warm gradient glow* — **no chapter-level color arc**. One deliberate **warm-accent beat at the logo (P5)**.
- **Through-line:** the **TIS isometric cube** sits at screen-center the entire film — the hub and the mark itself; at the end the generated cube crossfades to the real TIS submark (same form → seamless).
- **Negative prompt (paste every time):** `ink, paper, watercolor, paint, painted, brush, hand-drawn, sketch, grain, texture, canvas texture, splatter, Rubik's cube, colorful cube, rainbow, multicolored, text on faces, text, words, letters, numbers, typography, captions, watermark, logo, brand marks, arrows, connecting lines, charts, gibberish, deformed shapes, people, faces, hands, dark background, black background, neon, heavy glow, clutter, busy background`

### Hero section composition — headline + film *(PARKED — revisit before homepage build)*

**The insight.** A hero is a ~5-second explainer of *what TIS does*; this film is a ~30-second *atmospheric story*. Those are two different jobs. The film's beat 1 ("From patent intelligence → to capital. One partner.") is a **poster line**, not a 5-second explainer — by design, it withholds for intrigue. So the literal "what TIS is/does" must live in a **static headline + subhead** layered in the same hero section, not in beat 1. The headline also gets to do the one thing the film deliberately can't: use a **category noun** ("the IP firm…") so *what TIS is* stops being fuzzy — without burning the reserved "vertically integrated IP firm" term.

**Layout intent.** Film loops (muted) as the atmospheric layer; a static headline block (headline + one-line subhead + CTA) sits overlaid/adjacent so a scanner who never hits play still grasps TIS in 5s.

**Headline directions (draft — not locked):**

1. *Name the category + the move (fixes "what is TIS"):*
   - **The IP firm that grades patents — and backs them with capital.**
   - *Every patent graded against its peers, drawn from a 180M-patent database. Then owned, licensed, or invested in — one partner.*
2. *Plain-verb version (fixes "what does TIS do"):*
   - **Know what a patent is worth. Then put it to work.**
   - *TIS grades any patent against its industry peers, then owns, licenses, and invests across IP — as one partner.*
3. *Closest to the film, but explicit:*
   - **Patent intelligence, turned into capital.**
   - *We grade patents against a 180M-patent database, then own, license, and invest in IP — one partner, start to finish.*
4. *New positioning line (from [brand/positioning.md](../../brand/positioning.md) §3 — strongest category claim):*
   - **Patents are the most undervalued asset of our time.**
   - *We make their value seen, measured, and unlocked — starting with the rating engine that says what a patent is worth.* (Verb triad maps to the film: seen = intelligence, measured = the engine, unlocked = own/license/invest.)

**Open for this section:** lock a headline direction; confirm headline-vs-film layering (overlay vs stack); decide whether the category noun ("IP firm") is the public term or a placeholder.
