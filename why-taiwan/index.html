Why Taiwan, why now

Demand for unmanned systems in Taiwan is arriving faster than the capacity to sustain them, and 9 of 16 leading foreign UAV makers hold no Taiwan patents.

Request a scoping conversation

Demand is arriving faster than sustainment capacity.

Every one of those systems needs maintenance, spares, repair and a warranty organisation on the island.

48,750 aircraft in the Armaments Bureau's 2026⁠–⁠2027 procurement

21× growth in Taiwan UAV exports from 2024 to 2025

~2.5× growth in Taiwan UAV output value, NT$5.0 bn to NT$12.9 bn

Three programs behind the demand

    • 11,270 aircraft in 2026, 37,480 in 2027
    • Bought by the Armaments Bureau, multi-award
    • No PRC-origin components; domestic components preferred
    • NT$44.2 bn in total
    • Coordinated by the Ministry of Economic Affairs
    • Builds the domestic UV industry
    • NT$780 bn ceiling, passed May 2026
    • UAV and counter-UAS systems bought through foreign military sales
    • Foreign makers will field systems here that need local sustainment

The patent picture is thin

9 of 16 leading foreign UAV makers hold no Taiwan patents. Once systems are fielded they get studied, and without Taiwan filings a maker has no position against local imitation and no leverage with local suppliers.
