Why Taiwan, why now

Demand for unmanned systems in Taiwan is arriving faster than the capacity to sustain them, and 9 of 16 leading foreign UAV makers hold no Taiwan patents.

Request a scoping conversation
Photo: production line for UAV airframes

Demand

Demand is arriving faster than sustainment capacity.

  • 48,750aircraft in the Armaments Bureau's 2026–2027 procurement
  • ~21×growth in Taiwan UAV exports from 2024 to 2025
  • ~2.5×growth in Taiwan UAV output value, NT$5.0 bn to NT$12.9 bn

Every one of those systems needs maintenance, spares, repair and a warranty organisation on the island.

Three programs behind the demand

  • Military-use, commercial-spec procurement

    The Armaments Bureau is buying 11,270 aircraft in 2026 and 37,480 in 2027, multi-award, with no PRC-origin components and domestic components preferred.

  • UV industry development program

    NT$44.2 bn coordinated by the Ministry of Economic Affairs to build the domestic UV industry.

  • Defense special act

    Passed in May 2026 with an NT$780 bn ceiling. Its UAV and counter-UAS systems are bought through foreign military sales, so foreign makers will field systems in Taiwan that need local sustainment.

The patent picture is thin

9 of 16 leading foreign UAV makers hold no Taiwan patents. Once systems are fielded they get studied, and without Taiwan filings a maker has no position against local imitation and no leverage with local suppliers.

  • Island-wide field service and spares

    Automotive dealers with no UV competence, or aerospace shops with no island-wide network.

    A handful of shops, all capacity-constrained.

  • Export-control-aware operations

    Nobody packages it; each maker builds it ad hoc.

  • Taiwan and Asia patent strategy

    Patent firms paid by filing count; no UV-specialised advisor.

  • Licensing to local suppliers

    No intermediary.
