How Patent Search works

  1. 1

    Describe it.

    One sentence about what you're building. Press Search; there's nothing to set up.

  2. 2

    Read the results.

    Patents ranked by relevance, each with its status, holder, drawings and claims.

  3. 3

    Star and find similar.

    Star the closest ones. Find similar re-sorts the list around any patent you starred.

  4. 4

    Take it with you.

    Download your starred patents as a CSV or Markdown file.

Describe it your way, not in patent terms

Keyword search misses patents written in other words, and buries the close ones under dozens that aren't.

Patent Search starts from your sentence. No classification codes, no search syntax.

  • See what it searches

    Patent Search runs on Innovue's 180M-patent database, covering the USPTO, TIPO and other major offices. The app shows every source and when it was last updated.

  • 180M-patent databasethe full Innovue database
  • 6 patent officesUS, CN, TW, EP, KR, JP
  • Patents and applicationsgranted, published, or both

  • Semiconductor devices first, with more fields to come.

    Powered by

Set the scope before you search

  • Six patent offices.

    US, China, Taiwan, Europe, Korea and Japan, in any combination.

  • Patents and applications.

    Granted, published, or both.

  • Dates.

    Filter by filing or publication date.

  • Legal status.

    Live, expired, abandoned or pending. Filtering never reruns the search.

  • Every drawing.

    Open any figure full size, then zoom and rotate.

Read each patent without losing your place

Your results stay on the left while the full record opens on the right.

  • Status and holder
  • Inventors and classes
  • Every drawing, full size
  • The abstract and the claims

The score beside each result is the engine's relevance ranking. It says how close a patent is to what you described, not whether it affects you.

Take the data and draw your own conclusions

Download your starred patents as a CSV or Markdown file, with the same fields you see on screen and nothing added.

  • No verdicts. The engine's score and the patent's own text, nothing more.
  • No charts or summaries. You work the data in your own tools.
  • No claims about empty ground. An empty list describes the search, not the field.
  • No legal advice. Whether a patent stands in your way is a question for an attorney.
